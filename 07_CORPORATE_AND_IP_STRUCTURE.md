# SYDTEK LEARNING PLATFORM ARCHITECTURE
## 07 — CORPORATE & IP STRUCTURE

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** CANONICAL — LOCKED FOR INITIAL BUILD

---

# PURPOSE

This document defines the canonical corporate and intellectual-property structure for SydTek.

It establishes:

- parent-company logic
- operating-company separation
- intellectual-property ownership
- curriculum ownership
- trademark and domain ownership
- source-code ownership
- data ownership
- contractor and contributor assignments
- licensing principles
- separation of education, research, and experimental commercial activity
- public-company diligence hygiene

The purpose is not to create unnecessary legal complexity.

The purpose is to ensure that the business can answer clearly:

> Who owns what?

and:

> Which entity has the right to use, license, sell, modify, and protect each major asset?

---

# CORE PRINCIPLE

## CONTROL THE CORE. DISTRIBUTE THE RAILS.

SydTek may distribute through:

- Skool
- YouTube
- GitHub
- partner LMS systems
- employer systems
- social platforms
- mobile platforms
- future SydTek software

but the canonical intellectual property should remain under SydTek-controlled ownership or documented license.

The long-term corporate architecture should support:

**EDUCATION  
+ SOFTWARE

+ LICENSING  
+ INSTITUTIONAL SALES  
+ ENTERPRISE SALES  
+ FUTURE CAPITAL MARKETS**

without allowing ownership to become fragmented.

# RECOMMENDED TOP-LEVEL CONCEPT

Conceptually:

# SYDTEK HOLDINGS, INC.

Parent / holding entity

↓

## SYDTEK LEARNING

Primary education and learning-platform operating business.

↓

## SYDTEK LABS

Applied research, challenge, and experimental learning activity.

↓

## OTHER CONTROLLED OPERATING ENTITIES

Created only when justified by:

- law

- tax structure
- investor requirements
- regulatory obligations
- contractual needs

# IMPORTANT RULE

Do not create separate entities simply because every project has a different name.

Brands and products do not automatically require separate corporations.

Examples:

- SydTek Free
- SydTek Plus
- SydTek Campus
- SydTek Enterprise
- SydTek ONE

can all be product lines within one controlled company architecture.

A future parent company may hold or control:

- core trademarks
- curriculum IP
- source code
- data architecture
- learning graph
- knowledge graph
- institutional licenses
- major domains
- repositories
- product brands

The exact legal ownership structure should be finalized with qualified legal and tax counsel.

# OPERATING-COMPANY ROLE

Operating entities may handle:

- course sales
- subscriptions
- payroll
- contractors
- institutional contracts
- enterprise contracts
- customer support
- marketing
- learner operations

The parent and operating entities should have documented agreements where IP is licensed between them.

---

# IP HOLDING OPTIONS

Potential structures include:

## OPTION A — IP OWNED BY PARENT

Parent owns:

- trademarks
- curriculum
- platform software
- learning graph
- domains

Operating subsidiaries receive licenses.

This can simplify strategic ownership.

---

## OPTION B — IP OWNED BY PRIMARY OPERATING COMPANY

Primary operating company owns and commercializes IP directly.

This can be simpler at early stage.

---

# STRUCTURE RULE

Do not move valuable IP among entities casually.

Any transfer should be:

- documented
- authorized
- valued where required
- tax-reviewed
- contractually clear

Brand names do not necessarily equal legal entities.

Example:

**SydTek Campus**

It does not need its own company unless legally or operationally justified.

- credentials
- institutional funds

# DATA OWNERSHIP

SydTek should distinguish:

## COMPANY DATA

Operational and business records.

## LEARNER DATA

Personal and academic records.

## PARTNER DATA

Data governed by contract.

## RESEARCH DATA

Data governed by research terms.

## PUBLIC DATA

Information legally available for public use.

Ownership does not eliminate privacy obligations.

- employees

> You own 5%.

# EARLY-STAGE SIMPLICITY RULE

The structure should be sophisticated enough to protect the company but simple enough to operate.

Do not create:

**10 entities + 20 intercompany agreements**

before there is a real business reason.

Start with:

**CLEAR OWNERSHIP  
→ CLEAR CONTRACTS  
→ CLEAR ACCOUNTING  
→ CLEAR RECORDS**

Then add complexity only when justified.

# CORPORATE MIGRATION RULE

If the company later restructures:

**OLD ENTITY  
→ ASSIGNMENT / LICENSE  
→ NEW ENTITY**

Never assume ownership moved simply because the brand changed.

# FINAL CORPORATE RULE

SydTek should always be able to answer:

**WHO CREATED IT?**

**WHO OWNS IT?**

**WHO CAN USE IT?**

**WHO CAN LICENSE IT?**

**WHERE IS THE EVIDENCE?**

If the answer to any of these is unclear, the asset is not diligence-ready.
