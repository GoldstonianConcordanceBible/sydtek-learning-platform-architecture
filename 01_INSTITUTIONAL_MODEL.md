# SYDTEK LEARNING PLATFORM ARCHITECTURE
## 01 — INSTITUTIONAL MODEL

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** CANONICAL — LOCKED FOR INITIAL BUILD

# PURPOSE

This document defines the institutional structure of SydTek.

It establishes:

- what SydTek University is
- what SydTek Academy is
- what SydTek Scholars is
- how learning products relate to academic programs
- how noncredit, continuing-education, professional, and future credit-bearing activity are distinguished
- how institutional authority is represented publicly
- how research, laboratories, credentials, and partner institutions relate to SydTek
- how the platform can scale without confusing delivery technology with academic authority

---

# INSTITUTIONAL NORTH STAR

## SYDTEK TURNS LEARNING INTO VERIFIED HUMAN CAPABILITY.

SydTek is designed as a continuously updating learning institution and education technology platform.

The institution combines:

**EDUCATION  
→ APPLIED PRACTICE  
→ EVIDENCE  
→ ASSESSMENT  
→ PORTFOLIO  
→ CREDENTIAL  
→ RESEARCH  
→ WORKFORCE APPLICATION**

The objective is not merely to distribute information.

The objective is to help learners demonstrate what they can do.

---

# INSTITUTIONAL FAMILY

The SydTek institutional family consists of distinct but connected operating layers.

## 1. SYDTEK ACADEMY

Primary role:

- foundational education
- family learning
- youth-oriented learning
- introductory programs
- accessible public learning
- skill-building
- community education
- pre-professional exploration

SydTek Academy may include:

- K–12-oriented programs
- parent-and-child learning
- language programs
- foundational AI education
- creative learning
- introductory technology education
- educational pilots with community organizations

SydTek Academy should not imply college credit unless a specific authorized arrangement exists.

---

## 2. SYDTEK UNIVERSITY

Primary role:

- adult learning
- professional education
- applied higher-education-style curriculum
- advanced pathways
- stackable credentials
- interdisciplinary programs
- workforce development
- institutional courseware
- university partnerships
- professional and executive education

SydTek University is the primary home of the formal course-numbering architecture.

Examples:

- SYD-AI
- SYD-MKT
- SYD-W4
- SYD-SCM
- SYD-SUS
- SYD-GRT
- SYD-AIM
- DES

Programs may contain:

- free entry courses
- paid specialized courses
- stackable credentials
- laboratories
- capstones
- advanced professional programs
- institutional versions

---

## 3. SYDTEK SCHOLARS

Primary role:

- applied research
- scholarly publishing
- research translation
- case-study development
- evidence review
- public scholarship
- interdisciplinary research
- research partnerships
- conference and publication activity

SydTek Scholars may support:

**RESEARCH  
→ COURSE CONTENT  
→ CASE STUDIES  
→ LABS  
→ PUBLICATIONS  
→ NEW RESEARCH**

Research activity should distinguish between:

- peer-reviewed research
- working papers
- internal studies
- field observations
- case reports
- exploratory experiments
- proposed research

These categories must not be represented as equivalent.

# THE THREE-LAYER MODEL

The institutional system can be understood as:

## ACADEMY
**FOUNDATION**

↓

## UNIVERSITY
**APPLICATION + PROFESSIONALIZATION**

↓

## SCHOLARS
**RESEARCH + KNOWLEDGE CREATION**

These layers may interact.

Example:

**SydTek Scholars research**
→ becomes a University case study
→ becomes an Academy simplified learning activity
→ produces a new applied experiment
→ generates additional research.

# EDUCATIONAL DELIVERY MODEL

SydTek supports multiple forms of learning.

## PUBLIC LEARNING

Examples:

- YouTube
- social media
- free lessons
- public webinars
- open educational resources
- introductory Skool content

Purpose:

**DISCOVERY + ACCESS + COMMUNITY**

---

## NONCREDIT PROFESSIONAL EDUCATION

Examples:

- $29 self-paced courses
- professional certificates
- applied labs
- industry skill programs
- workforce development

Purpose:

**SKILL DEVELOPMENT + EVIDENCE OF CAPABILITY**

Unless otherwise formally authorized, these offerings should be described as noncredit professional education.

---

## CONTINUING EDUCATION

Continuing education may be introduced where applicable standards, instructional-hour requirements, recordkeeping, assessment, and provider requirements are satisfied.

Continuing education must be clearly distinguished from degree credit.

---

## CREDIT-RECOMMENDED LEARNING

Some mature courses may eventually undergo external review for college-credit recommendations.

A recommendation is not the same as automatic credit.

Receiving institutions determine whether they accept recommended learning for credit.

---

## PARTNER-AWARDED CREDIT

A college or university partner may determine that SydTek learning satisfies requirements within its own academic framework.

In such arrangements:

- the partner determines academic acceptance
- the partner controls its own credit
- the partner controls its own degree requirements
- SydTek supplies agreed curriculum, technology, instruction, assessments, or evidence

---

## FUTURE DEGREE PATHWAYS

SydTek may later participate in degree pathways through:

- articulation
- transfer arrangements
- co-developed programs
- university partnerships
- licensed courseware
- independently authorized degree-granting activity

Independent degree authority should be treated as a separate strategic decision rather than assumed by the existence of the platform.

# INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY PRINCIPLE

## PLATFORM CONTROL ≠ ACADEMIC AUTHORITY

Owning:

- course content
- a website
- a community
- a learning-management system
- an AI tutor
- a certification system

does not automatically establish:

- accreditation
- transfer credit
- degree authority
- state authorization
- university status in every legal context

Public descriptions should match actual authority.

---

# PUBLIC CLAIMS STANDARD

SydTek should use precise educational language.

Approved categories may include:

- free course
- professional course
- noncredit course
- professional certificate
- micro-credential
- continuing education
- externally evaluated
- credit recommended
- accepted for credit by a named institution
- partner-delivered
- accredited where formally applicable

Avoid unsupported blanket claims such as:

> “All SydTek courses are accredited.”

Instead state exactly:

- who accredited what
- which institution
- which program
- which credential
- which jurisdiction
- which period
- which scope

---

# COURSE AUTHORITY

Each SydTek course should have:

- canonical course ID
- canonical title
- program ownership
- version
- academic status
- free/paid status
- course owner
- learning outcomes
- completion requirements
- assessment requirements
- credential mapping

Course numbering should remain stable after publication.

---

# COURSE STATUS

Every course must have one of the following operational statuses.

## PLANNED

The course exists in the academic architecture but is not yet student-ready.

---

## BUILDING

The course is actively being assembled.

---

## LIVE

The course is available to learners.

---

## INSTITUTIONAL

The course has been upgraded for licensing or formal partner review.

An institutional course should normally include:

- syllabus
- learning outcomes
- instructional hours
- prerequisite definitions
- assessments
- grading or evaluation criteria
- rubrics
- capstone
- accessibility review
- faculty guide
- content rights review
- version history
- learner record requirements

---

## ARCHIVED

The course is no longer actively offered but remains part of the institutional record.

Archived courses should retain:

- course ID
- version history
- historical student records
- prior credential relationships

---

# COURSE NUMBERING PRINCIPLE

## COURSE IDS ARE PERMANENT ACADEMIC IDENTIFIERS.

Example:

`SYD-MKT-150`

should not casually become:

`SYD-MKT-205`

after students have completed the course.

Instead use:

`SYD-MKT-150 v1.0`

then:

`SYD-MKT-150 v1.1`

Stable identifiers support:

- transcripts
- certificates
- institutional review
- licensing
- learner records
- analytics
- accreditation preparation
- historical provenance

---

# CROSS-LISTING

A course may belong to multiple academic pathways.

Example:

`DES-109 / SYD-MKT-150`

This represents:

**ONE COURSE  
→ MULTIPLE CURRICULAR RELATIONSHIPS**

It does not automatically mean two separate sets of coursework.

Cross-listing should preserve:

- one canonical course record
- one version history
- one primary content package
- multiple approved program mappings

# LEARNING EXPERIENCE STANDARD

The SydTek learning experience is built around:

**LESSON  
→ PRACTICE  
→ OUTPUT  
→ ARTIFACT  
→ ASSESSMENT  
→ CAPSTONE**

The learner should increasingly be able to show evidence of capability.

A course completion record should ultimately answer:

> What did the learner demonstrate?

not merely:

> What content did the learner watch?

# CREDENTIAL ARCHITECTURE

SydTek credentials should be stackable.

Potential levels:

## COURSE COMPLETION

One course.

↓

## MICRO-CREDENTIAL

A focused cluster of demonstrated skills.

↓

## FOUNDATIONAL CERTIFICATE

Multiple courses within a coherent skill domain.

↓

## ADVANCED CERTIFICATE

Higher-level technical or professional capability.

↓

## PROFESSIONAL CERTIFICATE

A larger applied pathway with substantial assessment or capstone work.

↓

## INSTITUTIONAL ARTICULATION

Approved learning may map into a partner's academic structure.

---

# CREDENTIAL RULE

## A CREDENTIAL SHOULD RESOLVE TO EVIDENCE.

The long-term credential record should identify:

- learner
- credential
- issuing entity
- issue date
- courses
- competencies
- assessments
- capstone
- version
- verification status

Where possible, the credential should connect to selected portfolio evidence.

SydTek differs from a purely static course platform by continuously transforming real activity into learning material.

The model is:

**ACTIVITY  
→ DOCUMENTATION  
→ EVIDENCE  
→ CASE  
→ COURSE  
→ CHALLENGE  
→ STUDENT OUTPUT  
→ RESEARCH  
→ UPDATED COURSE**

This architecture allows curriculum to evolve alongside:

- AI
- markets
- technology
- supply chains
- sustainability
- publishing
- entrepreneurship

---

# LIVING LAB GOVERNANCE

Real projects can become educational cases, but the academic record must preserve:

- what was known at the time
- what was assumed
- what action occurred
- what evidence existed
- what outcome occurred
- what failed
- what succeeded
- what remains uncertain

Unsuccessful experiments must not be removed simply because they are unfavorable.

Failure can be instructional evidence.

Human instructors, reviewers, faculty, and subject-matter experts retain responsibility for:

- academic standards
- assessment rules
- evaluation
- curriculum approval
- learner disputes
- institutional claims
- governance
- credential authority

AI may assist with:

- drafting
- tutoring
- feedback
- retrieval
- personalization
- assessment support
- content mapping
- analytics
- recommendation

But:

## AI GENERATES. HUMANS GOVERN.

# AI TUTORING PRINCIPLE

AI tutoring should help learners reason rather than simply provide completed work.

The platform should increasingly support:

**QUESTION  
→ GUIDANCE  
→ PRACTICE  
→ ATTEMPT  
→ FEEDBACK  
→ RETRY  
→ DEMONSTRATION**

The AI layer should distinguish:

- tutoring
- assessment assistance
- answer generation
- academic misconduct

---

# ACADEMIC INTEGRITY

SydTek should maintain academic-integrity standards appropriate to the level and purpose of each offering.

Potential requirements include:

- attribution
- disclosure of AI use
- original work
- evidence preservation
- source verification
- plagiarism controls
- assessment identity checks where necessary
- research ethics
- data integrity

The use of AI is not itself prohibited.

Undisclosed substitution of AI output for required human performance may violate course requirements.

---

# ACCESSIBILITY

Institutional-ready courses should progressively support:

- captions
- readable transcripts
- accessible documents
- alternative text
- navigable materials
- reasonable format alternatives
- accessible assessments

Accessibility should be treated as product quality, not a late-stage add-on.

---

# QUALITY ASSURANCE

Each institutional-ready course should eventually undergo periodic review.

Review categories may include:

- accuracy
- currency
- learning outcomes
- assessment alignment
- accessibility
- rights
- learner performance
- learner feedback
- case-study currency
- AI-generated content review
- external standards

Recommended status:

**CURRENT  
→ REVIEW DUE  
→ UNDER REVIEW  
→ UPDATED  
→ ARCHIVED**

# SKOOL'S ROLE

Skool is currently an important delivery environment.

Its role is:

**COMMUNITY + COURSE DELIVERY + ENGAGEMENT**

Skool is not the sole canonical repository for:

- curriculum
- institutional rules
- IP
- version history
- learner architecture
- public-company governance

Canonical architecture remains under SydTek-controlled systems such as GitHub and other official records.

---

# GITHUB'S ROLE

GitHub serves as a canonical architecture and version-control rail.

It may contain:

- institutional architecture
- program architecture
- course definitions
- metadata schemas
- version histories
- research artifacts
- technical systems
- public documentation

GitHub is not itself the educational institution.

# INSTITUTIONAL DATA PRINCIPLE

The long-term SydTek platform should distinguish:

## PUBLIC CONTENT DATA

Public educational materials.

## ACADEMIC DATA

Courses, assessments, mastery, and credentials.

## PERSONAL LEARNER DATA

Protected learner records and identifiable information.

## RESEARCH DATA

Data governed by applicable research and privacy rules.

## BUSINESS DATA

Sales, contracts, customers, revenue, and operations.

These categories may require different access controls.
